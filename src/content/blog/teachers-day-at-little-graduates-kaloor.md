India marks Teacher's Day on 5 September. In a preschool, the day is smaller than the
speeches many of us remember from our own school years. It is a chance for young children
to notice the people who greet them each morning, sit on the floor beside a puzzle, and
stay until the last parent arrives.

At Little Graduates in Kaloor, that is the heart of it. Not a performance. A thank you.

## Why we mark the day at all

Three- and four-year-olds do not need a lecture on the history of the day. They need a
concrete way to say "I see you." Gratitude at this age is a gesture, not an essay. A folded
card. A flower. A song they already know, sung a little more carefully than usual.

Montessori teachers are guides more than performers. They spend the day close to the work,
not at the front of a room. Teacher's Day is one of the few times a child turns toward that
adult on purpose, instead of toward the material on the shelf. That small turn matters. It
is how a child begins to understand that learning is a relationship, not only a set of
activities.

The adult they are thanking is the same person who helps with a shoe, waits through a hard
goodbye, and notices when a child is ready for a new piece of work. Honouring that person
is a way of making the day visible — to the child as much as to the teacher.

## What the morning usually looks like

We keep Teacher's Day close to an ordinary school morning, so nothing feels staged.
Children who want to make a card or pick a flower are given time and simple materials. Some
will spend ten careful minutes colouring. Some will make a mark and be done. Both are fine.

There is no prize for the prettiest card. Teachers do not prompt a speech. If a child wants
to hand their work over quietly, that is the whole ceremony. The point is the offering, not
the audience.

This is a preview of how the day tends to feel here, not a recap of a morning that has not
happened yet. Every group, and every child, will do it a little differently. That is as it
should be.

## What this is not

It is not a stage show for photographs. It is not a lesson in "being grateful" delivered by
an adult who then waits for the correct answer. Young children honour people by doing
something with their hands. That is enough.

We also do not ask families to prepare anything elaborate. A bought gift is not the point,
and a child who arrives empty-handed has not missed the day. Presence is the real offering,
at school and at home.

## If you want to join in from home

A short note in the bag is welcome. A flower from a balcony pot. Nothing expensive, nothing
fussy. Children this age copy the size of the gesture. A small, sincere one teaches more
than a large, anxious one.

If your child does not want to make anything, do not push. They can still have a warm
morning. You can talk on the way to school about the person who helps them find their cubby
or sit with them at snack. Naming that person is already a kind of thank you.

## The short version

Teacher's Day at Little Graduates is children thanking the adults who spend the day with
them. Cards, flowers, a quiet moment. The rest of the morning stays ordinary on purpose.
